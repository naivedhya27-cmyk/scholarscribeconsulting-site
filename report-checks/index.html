Five checks on every footnote

Each reference in the report goes through the same five checks, in the same order.

  1. 1

    The cited work exists as cited

    Authors, title, year, journal or publisher, volume, pages and link.

  2. 2

    The source says what the report says

    The source is read in full, and the passage is found at the page cited.

  3. 3

    Every figure is recalculated

    Each number is worked out again from the cited data.

  4. 4

    Conclusions follow from the evidence

    Each conclusion is checked against its evidence and against the rest of the report.

  5. 5

    Corrections are checked too

    Where a report has been corrected, every change between versions is checked.

An evidence pack with one page for each issue

  1. The report’s words, with the page number
  2. The source’s words, with the page number
  3. What is wrong, explained in four or five lines
  4. The correct citation, with links to both documents

The rules for what counts as an issue are set before checking begins. Reports you send and the findings we return are kept confidential.

Evidence packIssue 3

Year and volume differ from the published record

Reference. Report p. 61

Report, p. 61

Okafor, Brandt and Iyer (2019). Workforce standards in home care. Volume 12, pages 33-41.

Published record

Okafor, Brandt and Iyer (2020). Workforce standards in home care. Volume 14, pages 33-41.

What is wrong

The article was published in 2020, in volume 14. The year and volume in the report point to a different issue of the journal.

Correct citation

Okafor, Brandt and Iyer (2020). Workforce standards in home care. Volume 14, pages 33-41.

Evidence packIssue 1

The figure differs from the source

Figure. Report p. 42, source p. 7

Report, p. 42

Most providers now meet the minimum staffing standard: 64 per cent did so in the 2021 survey.

Source, p. 7

Of the 112 providers surveyed, 64 met the minimum staffing standard (57.1%).

What is wrong

The report gives the number of providers, 64, as a percentage. The source reports 64 of 112 providers, which is 57.1 per cent.

Corrected wording

Most providers now meet the minimum staffing standard: 57 per cent did so in the 2021 survey (Hale and Ortiz 2021, p. 7).

Evidence packIssue 2

The quoted words are not in the source

Quotation. Report p. 18, source p. 3

Report, p. 18

The evaluation found that the programme “reduced waiting times in every district” (Lund 2022, p. 3).

Source, p. 3

Waiting times fell in four of the six districts and rose slightly in the other two.

What is wrong

The quoted words do not appear in the source. The source reports shorter waiting times in four of six districts and longer waiting times in two.

Corrected wording

The evaluation found that waiting times fell in four of the six districts (Lund 2022, p. 3).
